It runs the interview, then it marks the tape.

Four screens, start to finish. Every picture below is the real app, with notes in the margin.

  1. Pick a job, or paste your own

    Paste the advert for the job you are going for. No advert to hand? Choose Easy, Medium or Hard and press Another example: every click deals a different job, with a CV written for it.

    • The level sets the kind of job and how many questions you get: 3, 5 or 8.
    • Another example never repeats a job until you have seen every one at that level.
    • Every advert is measured, yours included, so you know how demanding it reads.
    • An example CV is used for that session only. Your own is never touched.

    Ctrl + Enter starts the interview.

  2. Answer out loud

    Each question is read aloud and shown on screen. Press Space, answer the way you would on the day, and press Space again when you finish. A thin answer earns a follow-up, the way a real interviewer would press.

    • Your words appear as you speak: a rough draft, not the transcript you are scored on.
    • The bars are your real microphone level. Too quiet for a few seconds and it says so.
    • Strong answers land in the green zone, one to one and a half minutes.
    • Space starts and stops an answer. Esc ends the session.

  3. Read your answer, marked up

    Every answer comes back like a checked essay: what worked highlighted, what to change underlined, a note beside each explaining why, and a line you could say instead.

    • Each answer gets a score, and the session gets one overall.
    • Green highlights are what worked. The note beside each says why, in your own words.
    • Fillers get a dashed line, so you can see how many there were.

  4. Hear the moment it happened

    Your recording, laid out as a tape. Every mark from the debrief sits on it at the second it happened, so you can hear exactly what a note is about.

    • Tap a tag to jump straight to that moment.
    • Click any word to hear it.
    • Green bands worked, orange ones need changing, rose ticks are fillers.

Built for the interview you have next week, not for a course.

If you have had one interview, or none, the hardest part is that you never hear yourself. You leave the room knowing it went badly without knowing which part.

This is the room you go to first: one question, a timer, and afterwards somebody showing you plainly what landed and what did not.

  • The graduateYou are about to graduate and the questions are competency ones you have never had to answer out loud.
  • The second languageEnglish is your second language and you want to hear how you come across before somebody else does.
  • The freezeYou freeze, or you ramble, and you would rather find that out in an empty room than in the real one.
  • The private oneYou will not upload video of yourself to a company you have never heard of, in order to practise.

The tape never leaves the room.

Every model runs on this computer: the transcription, the language model that writes and scores, and the camera analysis. There is no account, nothing is uploaded, and the camera is measured while you answer rather than filmed.

No video

Not stored and then deleted: never written. What the camera contributes is whether you stayed in frame and how steady you held position.

No upload

The server binds to your own machine's loopback address. Your answers do not cross a network.

No account

Nothing to sign up for, and nothing that identifies you. Close the tab and it is over.
